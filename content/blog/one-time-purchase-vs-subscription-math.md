"$20/month" sounds cheap. It's the framing every subscription tool uses, and it works, because $20 is genuinely a small number. But almost nobody evaluates a recording tool for one month — you evaluate it for as long as you keep making the show. So let's actually do the math over a realistic timeline instead of a single billing cycle.

## The numbers, plain

Here's what the popular remote-recording tools cost at their entry paid tier, monthly billing, as of this writing:

| Tool | Entry paid tier | Annual cost |
|---|---|---|
| Riverside.fm (Pro) | $24/mo | $288/yr |
| Zencastr (Standard) | $20/mo | $240/yr |
| SquadCast (Hobbyist) | $20/mo | $240/yr |
| Descript (Hobbyist, monthly) | $24/mo | $288/yr |
| Restream (Professional) | $39/mo | $468/yr |
| StreamYard (Core) | $44.99/mo | $540/yr |

And here's what those same tools cost over 3 years, still at entry tier, ignoring any plan upgrades you'll likely need along the way:

| Tool | 3-year cost |
|---|---|
| Riverside.fm | $864 |
| Zencastr | $720 |
| SquadCast | $720 |
| Descript | $864 |
| Restream | $1,404 |
| StreamYard | $1,620 |

Bloom Meeting, at $49 once: **$49**, full stop, at any of those timeframes.

## Why this isn't quite fair (and why it still matters)

To be fair to the subscription tools: some of what you're paying for is real. Cloud storage that doesn't depend on your laptop's hard drive. A hosted editing interface you can use from any device. Ongoing feature development funded by that recurring revenue. A team behind it you can email when something breaks.

Bloom Meeting doesn't try to be all of those things — it's a desktop app, your recordings live on your machine, and there's no cloud dashboard to log into from a different computer. If that tradeoff doesn't work for how you operate (a fully distributed team that needs cloud access to every recording from anywhere, for example), a subscription tool is the more honest fit.

But if you're one host, or a small team recording from your own machines, the cloud-dashboard argument mostly doesn't apply to you — you're paying the subscription price for infrastructure you don't actually need.

## The breakeven math, worked out

Take Riverside's Pro plan at $24/mo as the example, since it's the most direct comparison (same core workflow: local recording, per-person tracks, AI editing tools).

- Bloom Meeting: $49 total, ever.
- Riverside Pro: $24 × however many months you keep the subscription active.

$49 ÷ $24 ≈ **2.04 months**. That's the entire breakeven point. Every month after month two, you're strictly ahead with the one-time purchase — and that gap only widens the longer you keep recording.

Run the same math against StreamYard's Core plan ($44.99/mo) and the breakeven is under *one and a half weeks* of the subscription.

## What "own it forever" actually means here

The other side of the math that doesn't show up in a spreadsheet: with a subscription, the moment you stop paying, you stop having access — not just to new features, to the tool entirely. Old episodes you edited inside the platform, gone. Templates you built, gone. With a one-time purchase, the app you bought keeps working on the version you bought it at, indefinitely, whether or not you ever pay again.

For a podcast that might go on hiatus for a few months, or that only records seasonally, this matters more than the raw dollar figure. You're not paying rent on software you're not actively using.

## Bottom line

If you record remote interviews regularly and you're currently on a $20-45/mo tool, you've likely already spent more than $49 on it this year. The math isn't close after the second month.
